C1 — Section A · Media Language & Representation · 35 marks
Unseen extracts provided in exam each year
Music video extract (Q1.0) Print unseen + set text (Q2.0)
Q1.0 · 10m Media Language

Analyse visual elements in an unseen music video extract. AO2 only — no set product needed.

► Example question

Explore how visual elements create meaning in this music video extract. [10]

Q2.0 · 25m Representation

Compare representations in set product + unseen resource. Similarities, differences, contexts. Extended response.

► Example question

Compare representations in these two products. In your answer you must consider:

  • the choices the producers have made about how to represent
  • how far representations are similar in the two products
  • how far representations are different in the two products
C1 — Section B · Industries & Audiences · 25 marks
No Time to Die + 007.com Late Night Woman's Hour Black Panther
Q3.1 · 1m Q3.2 · 4m Industry knowledge

Name an organisation [1m] then briefly explain two characteristics or differences [4m].

► Example

Q3.1: Name the organisation that regulates film in the United Kingdom. [1]

Q3.2: Briefly explain two ways in which the internet has changed the film industry. [4]

Q3.3 · 10m Industry extended

Extended industry or context question referencing a set product. AO1.

► Example

Explain how the film industry uses convergence to promote films. Refer to the 007 website to support your points. [10]

Q4.1 · 1m Q4.2 · 10m Audience

Knowledge recall [1m] then extended audience question referencing a set product [10m]. Apply audience theory.

► Example

Q4.1: Name one way in which audiences can interact with the 007 website. [1]

Q4.2: Explain how audiences engage with film in the digital age. Refer to No Time to Die to support your points. [10]

C2 — Section A · Television in the Global Age · 20m
Peaky Blinders The Bridge
No extract — relies entirely on knowledge of your set programmes
Q1 · 20mExtended essay

Extended response on your set TV programme — representation, industry, audience or contexts. Must make a sustained argument.

► Example

Explain how production processes influence the success of television programmes. Refer to your set programme in your response. [20]

C2 — Section B · Magazines · 40m total
Big Issue Vogue
Q4.1 · 5mTheory explain

Briefly explain a named theory with example from your set magazine.

► Example

Briefly explain Gerbner's cultivation theory. Refer to your set edition to support your response. [5]

Q4.2 · 15mExtended analysis

Extended analysis of representations in your set edition. Must make judgements.

► Example

Explore how particular social groups may be misrepresented in your set edition. [15]

Q5 · 20mExtended essay

Extended question on audiences, theory evaluation or representations. Sustained argument required.

► Example

To what extent do audience responses to magazines change over time? Refer to your set edition in your response. [20]

C2 — Section C · Online Media · 20m total
Zoe Sugg / Zoella Thiiird
Q7/8a · 5mTheory explain

Briefly explain a named theory with example from your set online product.

► Example

Briefly explain Gauntlett's theory of identity. Refer to Zoe Sugg in your response. [5]

Q7/8b · 15mExtended analysis

“How far” question — extended analysis of representations or media language. Must make a clear judgement.

► Example

Explore how online media producers construct representations of themselves. Refer to Zoe Sugg in your response. [15]

Key patterns
→C1 Q1.0 uses an UNSEEN music video — no set product needed
→C2 Section A: answer for YOUR set programme only
→All extended questions reward sustained argument and applied theory
→C2 Sec B & C: always exemplify theory with your specific set product
