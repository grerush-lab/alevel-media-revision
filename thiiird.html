THIIIRD — the intersectional online magazine

The Eduqas online media set product (the alternative counterpart to Zoella): an independent, intersectional fashion, arts & culture magazine — website, print, podcast & events — founded by Rhona Ezuma in 2017 to amplify marginalised voices. Studied across Media Language, Representation, Media Industries and Audiences plus contexts.

"AMPLIFYING UNDERREPRESENTED VOICES · KEEP YOUR THIRD EYE OPEN"

Product & contextWhat is THIIIRD?

  • What: an independent, intersectional fashion/arts/culture magazine — print + website + the THIIIRD Waves podcast + a live-events series. Strapline: "Intersectional magazine celebrating culture, diversity and heritage — KEEP YOUR THIRD EYE OPEN."
  • Ethos: an activist magazine and "safe space" that centres people of colour, queer, trans and non-binary people and other underrepresented groups. Its manifesto is "informed by decolonial and intersectional feminist thought" (the personal is political).
  • Founder: editor-in-chief Rhona Ezuma (Black British Nigerian, London) founded it in 2017 after seeing whose stories mainstream publishing left out.
  • The triple "i": third-space theory (where marginalised and dominant cultures meet and new identities form), third-kid culture (children of migrants blending their parents' culture and their own surroundings), and the third eye (higher, more enlightened perception).

Framework 1 of 4Media Language

Masthead & house style

  • Masthead: simple sans-serif uppercase lettering = authority and confidence; the III as pillars connotes classical architecture (the Parthenon — order, democracy, education) and the triple meaning of the title — a distinct, instantly-recognisable brand identity.
  • House style (consistent print + web): a minimalist, modern layout — a serene purple background, big striking images and limited text, with bold blocks of contrasting colour (orange/green against purple). The scrolling strapline "AMPLIFYING UNDERREPRESENTED VOICES" anchors the images.
  • Rainbow palette across the colour blocks evokes the Pride flag — signalling LGBTQ+ inclusiveness. Thumbnails carry a short enigmatic caption that acts as clickbait / enigma code (Barthes). Pages use an above-the-fold / below-the-fold structure, with colloquial and culturally-specific codes (e.g. spelling "werk") to hail particular audiences.

Reading the lead image (denotation → ideology)

  • A low-angle MCU of a regal young Black model in a customised 1950s pillbox hat positions her as powerful and to be admired. Her purple eyeshadow and red scarf tie into the site's colours — a unified design.
  • Referential / postmodern codes: the hat is raised on a packet of Africa's Finest Plantain Fufu (a playful, "closed" cultural reference); her top carries the Chinese size-inclusive brand UOOYAA — signs of mixed cultural heritage intersecting in a new "third space".
  • Realism vs stylisation: most images are highly stylised / high-fashion, but some are deliberately candid, black-and-white or naturalistic to feel grounded and real. The site has little embedded video/audio (it is primarily a print-magazine site) but many hyperlinks — the hypermodality of the web.
Theory — Baudrillard: THIIIRD is a postmodern bricolage of voices and references — but it reverses Baudrillard's simulacra: instead of images with no "real", it champions every contributor's real lived experience.

Binary oppositions the site sets up (Lévi-Strauss):

multiculturalism vs ethnocentrism
acceptance vs discrimination
queer / LGBTQ+ vs heteronormativity
belonging vs isolation
alternative vs mainstream
non-binary gender vs the gender binary

Framework 2 of 4Representation

Amplifying the underrepresented

  • THIIIRD's whole purpose is representation: it selects and amplifies groups the mainstream rarely shows — Black models with vitiligo, plus-size and trans models, Afrobeat band Kokoroko, the Muslim women's skate group UKTIS, refugee footballer Fatima. Its three stated values are representation, inclusivity, critical celebration.
  • Ethnicity: diverse, positive and powerful — but not utopian; it also tackles racism and hard realities. It represents people in a decolonial way rather than the "othering"/colonial framing of much mainstream media.
  • Gender & LGBTQ+: a spectrum, not a binary — cis, trans and non-binary people, men embracing femininity (make-up, dresses), coming-out stories and honest coverage of homophobia/transphobia (e.g. the low-angle Munroe Bergdorf interview as a role model; the What is Gender? video series).
  • Self-representation: the women-run, Black-owned, diverse team shapes the diverse content — the representations reflect the people who make it.
Theory: Hall — counters "otherness" with "togetherness"; challenges stereotyping and the "burden of representation". Gauntlett — a wide range of identities to "pick and mix". Gilroy — decolonial thought: many valid diasporic identities. (bell hooks — intersectionality: race + class + gender.)

Framework 3 of 4Media Industries

Independent, not-for-profit, resourceful

  • Ownership: independent — not owned by a conglomerate — giving creative freedom and room to take risks. Women-run and Black-owned; profits are reinvested; most staff are voluntary/freelance, and Rhona works on it alongside a day job styling.
  • Funding (no single parent company): Rhona's own savings at launch; cultural grants (the Prince's Trust, Hackney Council); and partnerships/sponsored content. There is no banner advertising, but brands can sponsor articles (advertorials), which must be clearly labelled as paid (regulation — IPSO). In 2022, Nike paid THIIIRD to promote its London community initiatives.
  • Distribution & convergence: ~3,000 print copies per issue (several sell out), distributed by Ra & Olly (Falmouth) across 20+ countries / 3 continents and via Stack & magCulture; sold at art galleries (Tate Modern) to build a cultural brand. No paywall online; social links + the podcast drive traffic and revenue as print circulation falls.
  • Survival: when a distributor went bankrupt in the pandemic, THIIIRD ran an Instagram crowdfunding campaign (~£3k in pre-orders) to fund Issue #5, "Defiant Beauty" (now sold out).

Theory — Hesmondhalgh: the opposite of the risk-minimising, formatted conglomerate. THIIIRD shows the internet's democratisation / radical potential — Rhona saw a gap and built her own media form — though it stays resourceful and partly relies on partnerships. The team are Hesmondhalgh's "digital optimists". Context: UK journalism is still 92% white, which THIIIRD actively counters.

Framework 4 of 4Audiences

A diverse niche community

  • Target: loosely-defined, diverse niche audiences — "very people-of-colour centred, very women-led and queer" — plus anyone who loves art, music, fashion and wants a fairer society. Addressed as a community of like-minded people (cultured, educated — high cultural capital).
  • Targeting devices: a striking aesthetic; a mode of address that is both friendly/informal (colloquial, cultural codes) and confident/assertive; images of the team so you feel "part of the circle".
  • Participation: the site actively invites you to Contact us, submit ideas, send work or even work for the magazine — plus the crowdfunding that kept it alive. A real DIY culture.
  • Global reach: website + social (~6,000 Instagram followers; ~1.9k web readers/month, 2023) + the THIIIRD Waves podcast (originally on Soho Radio, ~400k reach; a takeover with all-female FOUNDATION FM) reach niche audiences nationally and globally.
Theory: Shirky — the "end of the audience": THIIIRD's readers are active participants who speak back, submit and co-create. Gerbner — cultivation: THIIIRD deliberately counters the dominant ideology's repeated patterns, cultivating awareness of inequality through a different "lens".
